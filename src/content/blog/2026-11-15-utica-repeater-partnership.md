MURPHYS, Calif. (Nov. 15, 2026) — Signal Integrity & Emergency Radio Response Alliance (S.I.E.R.R.A) 501(c)(3) announced today that the Utica Water and Power Authority (UWPA) Board of Directors voted to partner with the nonprofit to host solar-powered, off-grid emergency communications repeaters on Utica property.

The agreement allows S.I.E.R.R.A to install MeshCore repeaters at strategic Utica facilities, extending high-strength signal coverage across downtown Murphys. This expansion enables local residents, families, and businesses to connect to Calaveras County's decentralized emergency text-messaging network when commercial cellular and electrical grids fail.

"Partnering with the Utica Water and Power Authority is a major milestone for regional resilience," said Jay Goldberg, Founder and President of S.I.E.R.R.A. "Securing elevated, strategic host sites allows us to blanket downtown Murphys with reliable, off-grid text messaging so community members can reach loved ones and coordinate during emergencies regardless of power outages or cell tower failures."

In addition to public emergency communications, S.I.E.R.R.A and UWPA plan to evaluate off-grid mesh networking solutions to support backup utility telemetry and staff field communications during severe weather events and infrastructure disruptions.

S.I.E.R.R.A's network operates on low-power, long-range LoRa radio hardware paired with standard smartphones and MeshCore companion node via Bluetooth—requiring no cell service, internet connection, or commercial power. The system previously proved itself during the February 2026 snowstorms along the Highway 4 corridor, where the repeater network stayed up while telecommunications and power went dark.

## About Utica Water and Power Authority

The Utica Water and Power Authority (UWPA) is a public joint powers agency responsible for operating and maintaining the historic Utica water supply and hydroelectric system, delivering vital water resources and clean renewable energy to communities along the Highway 4 corridor in Calaveras County.
