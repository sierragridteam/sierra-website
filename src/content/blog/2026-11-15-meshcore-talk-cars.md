Jay Goldberg, KB6ENY, the founder and president of S.I.E.R.R.A, gave a presentation on MeshCore to the Calaveras Amateur Radio Society (CARS). He demonstrated the equipment, explained how a mesh network operates, and described the alliance's work building a network of solar-powered radio nodes.

## The club

The [Calaveras Amateur Radio Society](http://calaverasars.org/Web) is a local amateur radio club. It runs the N6FRG repeater and holds two weekly nets: the Monday Night Net at 19:30 on the repeater, 145.170 MHz, which the club describes as open to members "and everyone else," and a Monday-morning net at 09:00 on 7.198 MHz on the 40-meter band.

## What MeshCore is

MeshCore is software for small, low-power radios built on [LoRa](https://www.semtech.com/lora/what-is-lora), which its developer, Semtech, describes as a long-range, low-power wireless platform. The project describes itself as a [lightweight, open-source library for multi-hop packet routing](https://github.com/meshcore-dev/MeshCore): a message can pass from one radio to the next until it reaches the radio it is meant for. It needs no central server or internet connection, and it is released under the MIT License.

A MeshCore network has distinct roles. According to the project's [FAQ](https://docs.meshcore.io/faq/), a companion radio connects to a phone or web app and serves as a person's messenger. Companion radios do not repeat. Repeaters extend the network's range, and a repeater does not retransmit every packet it receives. A room server is a simple bulletin board that stores posts so users can read them later.

Jay is a retired law enforcement officer and an active member of the Calaveras County Sheriff's Search and Rescue team. He also assists the [Ebbetts Radio Safety Net (ERSN)](https://ersn.net/), the volunteer radio group S.I.E.R.R.A grew out of. His full profile is on the [About page](/about/jay).
